What makes it Kapiloka

Five little things,
done properly

01

Filter coffee, the real way

Strong, authentic South Indian filter coffee, served in traditional brass tumblers — hot enough to spark memories and conversations.

02

Mirchi Bajji — the star

Regulars name it the star item of the shop: crisp golden mirchi bajji, three pieces for ₹50, fried fresh through the day.

03

Fresh-fried snacks

Samosas, punugulu, masala wada and pakodas lined up in the glass display case — classic snacks, fresh and flavourful.

04

A corner of old Vayora

A vintage nook inside Alder Street Station — aged wooden tables, cane-back chairs and décor that feels like stepping into a slower decade.

05

Pocket-friendly, always

The whole board sits between ₹30 and ₹90 — great quality at prices regulars call the best part of the visit.

Step inside

Inside the
little shop

  • Brass tumblers, on the shelf

    Coffee arrives in a traditional brass tumbler and davara — and the shelves behind the counter gleam with brassware.

  • The sticky-note wall

    Hundreds of handwritten notes from visitors cover the walls — leave one of your own with your kaapi.

  • The brass bell corner

    A brass bell and framed artwork watch over the counter, with a small marigold shrine by the fridge.

  • Checkered floors, cane chairs

    Black-and-white checkered floor, dark-wood and cane-back chairs, a red-tiled canopy and marigold garlands overhead.
